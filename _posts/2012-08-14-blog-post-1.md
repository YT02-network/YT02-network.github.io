Reflection Question: After using Shodan's Search and Explore features to investigate internet-exposed ICS (Industrial Control Systems) devices and protocols, what surprised you most about the types of systems that are publicly accessible? How might this visibility pose a risk to critical infrastructure, and what steps should organizations take to reduce this exposure?
A: The thing that surprised me the most was the amount of information that was out in the public!! Although I do not yet have the knowledge or the skills to fully understand or fully utilize the information given on the site, I believe that in the proper hands, someone can really cause some damage with the information given. In order to lower the risks of an attack, I believe that these organizations should patch up these ports by adding more firewalls and other detection/prevention systems to the network. Although these ports are supposed to be open to the internet, some organizations had multiple ports open. This is a major security risk as these ports were most likely not as secure as other ports. If possible, I think it is in the best interest of the organization to have these ports completely inaccessible from the internet. Since the devices I saw were from industrial IoT, it is better to have connectivity to these devices completely private. Although this keeps accessibility to the device confined to on-site, I believe that the dangers of an attack outweigh the convenience of off-site management.
